# Observatory Charter

**Initiative:** The Omniversal Knowledge Observatory  
**Archive parent:** ScrollVerse Omniversal Manifesto  
**Foundation accession date:** 2026-11-16  
**Status:** Charter established in this proposed foundation; operations and governance remain to be implemented.

## Mission

Build a durable, interdisciplinary observatory that can discover, track, document, compare, and responsibly advance inquiry into phenomena and knowledge traditions—especially where evidence is scattered, contested, unfamiliar, culturally sensitive, or prematurely dismissed.

The Observatory does not preselect a conclusion. It preserves what was observed, what was claimed, what can be checked, what remains unknown, and how understanding changes.

## Commitments

1. **No premature dismissal.** Unfamiliarity is not evidence of uselessness or falsity.
2. **No premature acceptance.** Possibility, testimony, symbolism, and intuition are not automatically proof of a factual mechanism.
3. **Originals first.** Preserve original source material and its metadata before creating summaries or interpretations.
4. **Plurality without flattening.** Different disciplines and traditions retain their own methods, vocabulary, and standards.
5. **Independent corroboration.** Repetition of one source by many outlets is not independent confirmation.
6. **Transparent uncertainty.** Record gaps, contradictions, failed tests, and alternative explanations.
7. **Cultural respect.** Indigenous and community-held knowledge is not free raw material. Follow consent, attribution, access restrictions, and community protocols.
8. **Safety and legality.** No trespass, dangerous experiments, harassment, doxxing, or interference with aircraft, satellites, emergency services, or protected sites.
9. **Reproducibility where possible.** Preserve methods, instruments, parameters, data, and analysis steps.
10. **Versioned understanding.** New evidence may change a conclusion; it must not silently rewrite the historical record.

## Six domains and their specialist lenses

### I. Ancient civilizations and knowledge traditions
Archaeology, epigraphy, linguistics, archaeometry, history of science, manuscript studies, oral history, and Indigenous methodologies. Record original language, translation lineage, dating method, excavation context, and modern attribution separately.

### II. Religious developments and spiritual movements
History of religions, theology, anthropology, sociology, textual criticism, and lived-practice testimony. Represent beliefs accurately and respectfully. Do not adjudicate theological truth through scientific labels.

### III. UAP disclosure
Official records, FOIA releases, sensor provenance, aviation and atmospheric science, astronomy, radar/IR analysis, pilot testimony, and independent technical review. Keep event identification separate from speculation about origin.

### IV. Unexplained phenomena
Witness interviews, historical records, environmental measurements, meteorology, geology, ecology, psychology, and forensic analysis. Consider ordinary explanations carefully and record when they do not adequately explain the available details.

### V. Cosmic discoveries
Peer-reviewed astronomy, observatory datasets, mission archives, spectroscopy, planetary science, astrobiology, and biosignature research. Distinguish candidate detections, false-positive analysis, and confirmed discoveries.

### VI. Consciousness and intelligence
Neuroscience, cognitive science, psychology, philosophy of mind, comparative cognition, AI evaluation, and first-person reports. Separate observable behavior, self-report, computational capability, proposed mechanism, and philosophical interpretation.

## Governance model

The proposed review structure has four distinct functions:

- **Accession steward:** checks metadata, preservation, rights, and source lineage.
- **Domain reviewer:** evaluates methods and evidence within the relevant discipline.
- **Independent reviewer:** checks corroboration, alternative explanations, and conflicts of interest.
- **Cultural/community advisor:** consulted where knowledge, sites, practices, or testimony belong to a living community.

A single person may perform more than one role during the foundation phase, but the record must disclose that fact. A reviewer is not represented as independent when they are also the source, author, sponsor, or advocate of the claim.

## Outputs

- Searchable discovery register.
- Original-source and derivative-artifact catalog.
- Dated evidence and interpretation histories.
- Domain reviews and cross-domain research briefs.
- Reproducible methods and data packages where lawful.
- Public summaries that link back to preserved source records.
- Explicit unresolved-question queues.

## Success criteria

The Observatory is progressing when another researcher can locate the original source, reconstruct why a claim was made, inspect the evidence and counter-evidence, understand what changed, and identify what remains to be tested—without relying on the Observatory's authority alone.
