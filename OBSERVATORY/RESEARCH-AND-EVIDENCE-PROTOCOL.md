# Research and Evidence Protocol

## 1. Accession before interpretation

At first capture, assign a stable accession ID and record:
- capture timestamp in ISO 8601 UTC;
- original publication or event date, if known;
- source URL, archive identifier, repository path, or physical collection reference;
- source creator, witness, institution, or custodian;
- source type and media format;
- geographic origin, including uncertainty and precision;
- access date and access conditions;
- file size and SHA-256 hash when bytes are preserved;
- copyright, license, consent, privacy, and cultural restrictions.

Do not infer an event date from an upload date. Do not infer a location from a witness's general region. Mark unknown values as unknown.

## 2. Keep the layers separate

Every record may contain these separately labeled layers:

- **RAW SOURCE:** faithful source copy, transcript, or exact citation. Never silently edited.
- **TRANSCRIPTION / TRANSLATION:** derivative with method, translator, language, and uncertainty noted.
- **CONTEXT:** historical, geographic, institutional, and cultural background.
- **OBSERVATION:** what a witness, instrument, or document directly records.
- **CLAIM:** what someone says the observation means.
- **ANALYSIS:** tests, comparisons, and reasoning.
- **INTERPRETATION:** a proposed explanation or meaning.
- **REVIEW:** independent or specialist assessment.
- **REVISION HISTORY:** dated additions, corrections, disagreements, and status changes.

A summary is a navigation aid, never a replacement for the source.

## 3. Corroboration standard

For each corroborating item, state:
- what exact detail it supports;
- whether it is genuinely independent;
- whether it derives from the same witness, press release, dataset, or original report;
- whether it was collected before or after the claim became public;
- whether the methods and chain of custody are inspectable.

Ten articles repeating one press release count as one source lineage, not ten independent confirmations.

## 4. Competing explanations

List plausible explanations before selecting a preferred one. For each, state:
- expected observations;
- evidence for and against;
- tests already performed;
- tests not yet possible;
- what result would change the assessment.

Do not force a false balance: explanations with very different evidentiary support should be described accordingly.

## 5. Evidence states are claim-specific

Use the state vocabulary in the Observatory README. A record can contain multiple claims with different states. Example: a video file may be verified as authentic bytes, while the identity of the object shown remains unresolved.

## 6. Disciplinary method

- **Ancient knowledge:** cite excavation reports, museum catalogues, inscriptions, manuscript witnesses, critical editions, language specialists, and oral-history custodians. Clearly distinguish ancient source from modern esoteric attribution.
- **Religion:** cite primary texts and tradition-specific scholarship; distinguish historical claims, theological beliefs, personal testimony, and institutional determinations.
- **UAP:** preserve original files and metadata where available; document sensor type, calibration, geometry, weather, flight context, and data limitations. Do not infer extraterrestrial origin from unidentified status.
- **Unexplained phenomena:** document witness conditions, environmental context, instrument limitations, and ordinary hypotheses. Avoid unsafe fieldwork and trespass.
- **Cosmic science:** cite observatory or mission datasets and peer-reviewed analysis; document calibration, signal-to-noise, false-positive models, and replication status.
- **Consciousness/intelligence:** separate first-person report, behavioral performance, neural correlate, computational description, and metaphysical interpretation. Do not claim that one category conclusively settles another.

## 7. Cultural and ethical safeguards

- Seek permission before publishing restricted, sacred, or community-held knowledge.
- Attribute knowledge to its originating people or custodians where appropriate.
- Do not treat Indigenous knowledge as anonymous public-domain data.
- Minimize personal data about witnesses; obtain consent for identifiable interviews or recordings.
- Do not publish exact coordinates for vulnerable archaeological, sacred, ecological, or private sites when disclosure could cause harm.
- Do not encourage self-experimentation, medical substitution, dangerous rituals, or hazardous observation methods.

## 8. Preservation and integrity

- Store originals read-only where feasible.
- Generate SHA-256 hashes for captured files and record the tool and time used.
- Preserve original file names and metadata; store normalized derivatives separately.
- Keep append-only revision records.
- Back up across independently administered storage locations.
- A hash proves byte-level identity against a known digest, not authorship, provenance, truth, or the date an event occurred.
- Respect source licenses and access restrictions; link rather than copy when redistribution is not authorized.

## 9. Updating a record

When new evidence arrives:
1. Create a dated revision.
2. Preserve the previous state and rationale.
3. Identify exactly which claim changed.
4. Explain the new evidence and its independence.
5. Record dissenting review where relevant.
6. Update the public summary without deleting the historical version.

## 10. Publication checklist

Before a discovery is described as established:
- source lineage is documented;
- original evidence is accessible or its access limitation is explicit;
- independent corroboration is evaluated;
- competing explanations are recorded;
- disciplinary reviewers and conflicts are disclosed;
- limitations and unresolved questions are visible;
- public wording matches the evidence state.

**Core rule:** Be open enough to investigate the unfamiliar and rigorous enough not to overstate what the investigation found.
